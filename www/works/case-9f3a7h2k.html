Overview

A discovery-driven rebuild, owned end to end for over four years.

TVEyes delivers near real-time transcripts, metadata, audio and video from nearly 3,000 channels across 27 countries. Its users are PR analysts, communications teams and crisis teams who watch their organisations' presence across global media every minute.

AgileEngine engaged me to lead a full design program. Not a redesign sprint, but a rebuild with a design system that would outlive the project and a hand-off rigorous enough to support React and MUI development end to end.

I came in as one designer and analyst, ran discovery with stakeholders and users, built the strategy and backlog, and led the product for years. The work then expanded into a second iteration, the Twosion AI Release: sentiment analysis, smart alerts, accessibility mode and AI-powered entities on top of the foundation laid in phase one.

The problem

A 25-year-old product carrying outdated UX patterns into a real-time AI era.

The legacy product had grown organically over two decades. Power was high, usability had not kept up. Users couldn't filter by sentiment, share clips fluently, or work from the field.

There was no design system, and every release widened the inconsistency. Discovery interviews, system audits and CJM workshops surfaced the same patterns repeatedly.

Primary persona

Larry, a Public Relations Analyst

Forty, at a small New York agency. His day is media monitoring: tracking client mentions across thousands of channels, catching negative coverage before it spreads, and packaging clips and reports for clients.

Every later design decision was checked against Larry's day, so the product solved real work and not assumptions.

Four problems we refused to ship without solving
  • Old design with outdated UX patterns
  • Users can't schedule alerts by sentiment
  • Users don't know which mentions are negative
  • Users can't share events or reports
The solution

One Material-based system, four problems solved, three platforms.

We narrowed the program to the four problems and tied every later design decision to one of them. The answer was a single, owned design system and the flows built on top of it.

Solutions shipped
  • Design system based on Material UI
  • Smart alerts: spike, gap, and sentiment triggers
  • Sentiment analysis surfaced on every event
  • Sharing across clips, events and reports
Twosion AI Release

The second iteration: AI-powered entities, sentiment, and smart alerts.

Once the foundation was stable in production, we ran a design spike on AI features. Discovery, prototyping and high fidelity in compressed cycles. Sentiment analysis, smart alerts and AI entities were added without breaking the design language.

Smart Alerts. Spike detection, gap detection and sentiment shift detection. An analyst is alerted to a developing story before they refresh a search.

Sentiment Analysis. The emotional tone of mentions per keyword. Day-by-day score, distribution between positive, neutral and negative, with peak markers for live coverage.

Accessibility Mode. High-contrast mode meeting WCAG 2 AA, validated with heatmap analysis so users find primary actions equally well in both modes.

Heatmap-Driven Iteration. Every release iteration validated through heatmap analysis. Data-driven decisions, enhanced usability, streamlined navigation.

Weeks of discovery before a single solution was drawn.

We mapped the whole analyst journey in Miro first, then pressure-tested it in workshops and interviews. Nothing moved to prototyping until the problems were named, mapped, and agreed with the client.

Discovery did the heavy lifting. Systems evaluation, competitor analysis, user and stakeholder interviews, the Customer Journey Map, and information architecture. Decisions were defensible because they traced back to a discovery artifact, not a preference.

High fidelity was built entirely on the design system, so hand-off was a matter of linking libraries, not redrawing screens. The program did not end at release: Twosion AI extended the same foundation, and documentation kept the product team moving without me in the room.

Design System

A Figma library built on Material UI. Atomic, accessible, owned by the product.

Every button, icon, and input field is an atomic element. Branding, visual hierarchy, and WCAG 2 Level AA contrast were built into the tokens, not patched at the page level.

"A Figma file serving as TVEyes' own library of meticulously designed and developed components. It ensures consistency across applications and follows typical UX patterns, allowing for faster design and development."Hand-Off Report, Design System summary, June 2023
